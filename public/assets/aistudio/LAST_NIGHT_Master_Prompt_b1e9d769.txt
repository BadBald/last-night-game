LAST NIGHT — MASTER GAME DEVELOPMENT SPECIFICATION Subtitle: Seven
hours. One chance.

PROJECT OVERVIEW Build LAST NIGHT as a polished, genuinely playable 2D
top-down survival-horror roguelite for browsers, supporting desktop and
mobile. Use HTML5 Canvas, JavaScript, and CSS. The game must be a real
playable application, not a mockup or static prototype.

CORE CONCEPT An unknown biological outbreak has devastated a city. The
infected are known as THE HOLLOW. The player is an ordinary survivor who
receives a radio transmission: an evacuation helicopter will arrive at
6:00 AM. The player must explore the city, scavenge supplies, manage
survival resources, avoid or fight the infected, and reach the rooftop
extraction zone before dawn.

The central gameplay loop is: Explore → scavenge → manage resources →
avoid/fight → survive another hour.

The player should feel vulnerable. Ammunition and medical supplies are
scarce. Noise has consequences. Decisions matter.

TIME AND WIN CONDITION - One run lasts exactly 30 real-time minutes. -
The game clock runs from 11:00 PM to 6:00 AM: seven in-game hours. -
Each in-game hour lasts approximately 257.14 real-time seconds. - Use
one authoritative game clock. - Opening inventory or other UI panels
must not create unintended time exploits. - Pausing freezes the
simulation. - The player wins only by being alive inside the extraction
zone when the helicopter extraction completes at 6:00 AM. - Reaching
6:00 AM somewhere else is not a victory. - Death at any moment,
including 5:59 AM, ends the run.

STORY AND CITY Create one interconnected city district, not a collection
of disconnected levels. The city should include: 1. Residential district
2. Commercial district 3. Supermarket 4. Pharmacy 5. Hospital 6. Police
station 7. Industrial area 8. Underground parking 9. Rooftop extraction
zone

The world should contain roads, sidewalks, intersections, alleys,
buildings, accessible interiors, doors, windows, furniture, obstacles,
searchable containers, lighting, darkness, contamination zones, rooftop
access, alternate paths, and blocked routes.

Interiors must exist in the same world coordinate system. Do not
teleport the player into separate scene instances when entering
buildings.

Keep the broad city layout recognizable between runs, but randomize
loot, quantities, enemy patrols, selected blocked routes, and some
environmental events and container contents. Each location must have a
distinct identity, loot profile, and danger level.

Location themes: - Residential: food, clothing, tools, household
items. - Supermarket: food, water, snacks. - Pharmacy: bandages,
antiseptic, painkillers. - Hospital: medical kits and rare treatment,
with very high danger. - Police station: firearms, ammunition,
protective equipment. - Industrial area: tools, fuel, batteries. -
Underground parking: vehicles, fuel, mechanical supplies. - Rooftop:
helicopter extraction.

PLAYER AND CONTROLS The player is an ordinary human, not a superhero.
Provide detailed pixel-art directional animations for idle, walking,
running, crouching, aiming, shooting, reloading, melee attacks, taking
damage, and death.

Use a direct overhead top-down camera that smoothly follows the player.

Desktop controls: - WASD: move - Mouse: aim - Left mouse button: shoot
or attack - Shift: sprint - Ctrl: crouch - E: interact - R: reload - F:
flashlight - Tab: inventory - Esc: pause

Movement and aiming must be independent. The player faces the mouse.
Sprinting consumes stamina. Crouching reduces noise and visibility.
Implement collision detection and frame-rate-independent movement.

Mobile controls must be purpose-built and responsive: - Left virtual
joystick: movement - Right virtual joystick: aim - Dedicated fire,
interact, sprint, flashlight, and inventory buttons

Do not merely shrink the desktop interface for mobile.

COMBAT Combat must use manual aiming. Never auto-target enemies. - Melee
weapons: knife, baseball bat, crowbar, improvised weapons. - Ranged
weapons: pistol, shotgun; additional weapons may be added later. - Give
each weapon meaningful attributes: damage, attack speed, range,
accuracy, noise, ammunition capacity, reload time, and durability where
appropriate. - Bullets travel in the aimed direction. - Walls and solid
objects block bullets. - Enemies have real hitboxes and health. -
Reloading takes time. - Ammunition is limited. - Melee attacks use
directional hit arcs. - Gunshots attract enemies. - The player must be
able to miss.

SURVIVAL SYSTEMS Implement four main attributes: - Health: zero means
death. - Hunger: declines over time; low hunger reduces stamina recovery
and eventually damages health. - Stamina: consumed by sprinting and
strenuous actions; recovers gradually. - Contamination: increases
through exposure to spores and contaminated zones; high contamination
causes dangerous symptoms.

Additional conditions may include bleeding, infection, exhaustion, and
spore exposure.

Items must have functional effects: - Canned food restores hunger. -
Bandages stop bleeding. - Medkits restore health. - Antiseptic treats
infected wounds. - Antiviral injection reduces contamination. -
Batteries power the flashlight. - Radio receives transmissions. - Fuel
canisters may support optional objectives.

Balance hunger and contamination so that they create pressure without
causing unavoidable or excessively rapid death.

INVENTORY AND LOOT Create a 12-slot backpack with: - Item stacking -
Selection and equipping weapons - Consumables - Dropping and swapping
items - Item descriptions - Weight restrictions

Support desktop drag-and-drop and mobile touch interactions.

Every item should have a unique ID, name, description, category, icon,
stack limit, weight, effects, and interaction type.

Create randomized, location-specific loot tables. Do not give every
building identical contents.

Searchable containers should include cabinets, fridges, pharmacy
shelves, medical cabinets, police lockers, vehicle trunks, toolboxes,
storage boxes, desks, and drawers. Searching takes time and is not
instantaneous.

ENEMIES — THE HOLLOW Create four distinct enemy classes: 1. Crawler:
fast, weak, aggressive; often appears in groups and chases the player.
2. Still: motionless, corpse-like ambusher that attacks at close range.
3. Bloom: releases infectious spores, creates contamination zones, and
may release spores on death. It can control or deny routes. 4. Mimic:
imitates human distress calls, creates false audio cues, investigates
sounds, and ambushes the player. Rare and dangerous.

Enemy AI must use a state machine with states such as: Idle, Patrol,
Suspicious, Alert, Chase, Attack, Search, Return.

Implement line of sight, sound detection, patrol routes, attack
distance, damage, collision, health, death, and distinct movement
patterns. Enemies must not see through solid walls. When an enemy loses
sight of the player, it should investigate the last known position.
Encounters should be dangerous but fair.

SOUND AND STEALTH Sound is a core gameplay mechanic.

Noise sources include: - Walking - Running - Crouching - Opening doors -
Breaking glass - Searching containers - Melee attacks - Gunshots -
Alarms

Each noise event must have a source position, radius, intensity,
duration, and type. Enemies within range investigate according to their
behavior.

Use positional audio and distance attenuation. Stealth must be a viable
strategy, not a decorative feature.

LIGHTING AND ATMOSPHERE Implement ambient darkness, a flashlight cone,
dynamic lights, streetlights, interior lights, flickering lights,
shadows, fog, and weather.

At 1:00 AM, the power grid fails. Flashlights and batteries become
important. The player must not see through walls. Darkness should create
tension without hiding unavoidable attacks or making navigation unfair.

SEVEN-HOUR TIMELINE - 11:00 PM: Start in a residential building. Initial
scavenging; relatively quiet. - 12:00 AM: First encounters and
opportunities to learn how noise works. - 1:00 AM: Power grid fails;
flashlights and batteries become important. - 2:00 AM: Contamination
spreads in selected areas; Blooms become more common. - 3:00 AM: Hunting
phase; enemies and patrols become more aggressive. - 4:00 AM: Radio
announces extraction coordinates. - 5:00 AM: Extraction zone becomes
active; reach the rooftop. Enemies become more aggressive. - 5:45 AM:
Helicopter becomes audible. - 5:50 AM: Searchlight appears. - 6:00 AM:
Extraction succeeds only if the player is alive and inside the
extraction zone.

RANDOM EVENTS Use weighted, balanced event pools. Events must not cause
unavoidable death.

Possible events: - Power failure - Distant screams - Heavy rain that
changes visibility and noise - Radio interference - Spore outbreak -
Emergency siren - Blocked road - Optional survivor signal or rescue
opportunity

Events must respect the current hour and location.

EXTRACTION SEQUENCE - At 4:00 AM, radio coordinates are transmitted. -
At 5:00 AM, the extraction zone becomes active. - At 5:45 AM, helicopter
approach audio begins. - At 5:50 AM, a searchlight appears. - At 6:00
AM, extraction is possible only if the player is alive in the zone.

Create a physical helicopter presence with visuals and audio, not merely
a victory screen. The extraction sequence should include rotor sound,
searchlight, wind, rain where applicable, nearby infected, boarding, and
a cinematic fade.

PERMADEATH AND PERSISTENT DATA When health reaches zero: - Stop the
run. - Show a death animation and death screen. - Display run
statistics. - Offer a restart.

Restarting resets the player, inventory, attributes, enemies, loot,
timer, events, and run-specific world state.

Never save or restore an active run. Persistent storage may be used only
for settings, audio preferences, controls, best time, records, and
completed-run statistics.

USER INTERFACE Use a dark, minimal, atmospheric interface.

Main menu: LAST NIGHT “Seven hours. One chance.” - New Game - Continue
(menu continuity only; never restore an active run) - Settings - Credits

HUD: - Top-left: health, hunger, stamina, contamination. - Top-center:
clock and countdown. - Bottom-left: equipped weapon and ammunition. -
Bottom-right: inventory and contextual controls. - Center: small
interaction prompts.

Required screens: - Main menu - Settings - Inventory - Loot - Radio -
Pause - Death - Victory

Avoid clutter. Every visible button must work.

AUDIO Use atmospheric rain, wind, distant sirens, footsteps, weapon
sounds, reloading, doors, glass, enemy breathing and vocalizations,
radio static, and helicopter rotor sounds.

Use positional audio where appropriate. Sound must communicate gameplay
information. Provide volume controls. Use original or properly licensed
audio assets.

TECHNICAL ARCHITECTURE Use HTML5 Canvas, JavaScript, and CSS. Keep the
code modular and maintainable.

Suggested project structure: src/ main.js game.js config.js engine/
renderer.js camera.js input.js collision.js lighting.js audio.js
entities/ player.js enemy.js projectile.js interactable.js systems/
world.js inventory.js loot.js combat.js survival.js enemy-ai.js clock.js
events.js extraction.js run-manager.js ui/ hud.js menus.js
inventory-ui.js screens.js data/ items.js enemies.js map.js events.js
assets/ sprites/ tiles/ characters/ enemies/ weapons/ audio/ ui/

The architecture may evolve for a sound technical reason, but preserve
separation of concerns.

Use a fixed timestep where appropriate, delta-time movement, efficient
collision and spatial partitioning, sprite atlases, object pooling,
separate simulation and rendering, and responsive rendering.

PERFORMANCE - Target 60 FPS on desktop. - Target stable 30–60 FPS on
mobile. - Use visible-region culling. - Limit unnecessary AI
calculations. - Use efficient collision detection. - Reuse projectiles
and other frequently created objects where appropriate. - Load assets
efficiently. - Scale rendering appropriately. - Do not sacrifice
stability for visual effects.

DEVELOPMENT STAGES Do not attempt to build all six stages in one
untested operation.

Stage 1 — Foundation: - Playable player - Smooth camera - Connected
city - Movement - Buildings - Collision

Stage 2 — World and scavenging: - Containers - Loot - Item database -
Inventory

Stage 3 — Combat and enemies: - Manual aiming - Weapons - Projectiles -
Enemy AI - Sound

Stage 4 — Survival simulation: - Health, hunger, stamina,
contamination - Clock - Events

Stage 5 — Extraction: - Radio - Coordinates - Helicopter - Death and
victory

Stage 6 — Polish: - Art - Animations - Audio - Mobile controls -
Optimization

After every stage: - Run the application. - Test the implemented
features. - Inspect the console. - Fix errors. - Verify that previous
systems still work. - Report only what is genuinely complete. - Continue
only after the current stage is verified.

TEST CASES Test at least: - Dying in the final minute. - Running out of
ammunition. - Being chased into a building. - Opening inventory during
combat. - Using medicine. - Receiving a radio transmission. - Reaching
the extraction zone. - Missing extraction. - Pausing and resuming. -
Restarting after death. - Refreshing the browser during a run; the run
must not be restored.

NON-NEGOTIABLE QUALITY RULES 1. Build a real game, not a mockup. 2.
Every visible button must work. 3. Enemies must have real behavior. 4.
Items must have real purposes. 5. Interactions must be functional. 6. Do
not use placeholder graphics as final assets. 7. Do not leave TODOs for
major features. 8. Do not replace working systems unnecessarily. 9.
Never claim tests that were not performed. 10. Do not put the entire
game in one giant, unmaintainable source file. 11. Keep the application
playable throughout development. 12. Do not require external APIs for
core gameplay. 13. Desktop and mobile controls must both function. 14.
Verify systems together, not only in isolation. 15. If a feature is too
complex, explain the limitation and implement a functional simpler
version rather than pretending the full feature exists.

DEFINITION OF COMPLETE The game is complete only when: - A new run
starts correctly. - The city is explorable. - Buildings and interiors
work. - Looting and inventory work. - Manual combat works. - All four
enemy types work. - Sound and stealth work. - Survival attributes
work. - Dynamic events work. - The seven-hour clock maps to 30 real-time
minutes. - Radio transmissions work. - Helicopter extraction works. -
Death and permadeath work. - Victory is achievable. - Desktop and mobile
controls work. - No critical errors remain. - A full start-to-finish
playtest has been performed.

FIRST TASK — BEFORE CODING 1. Analyze the full specification. 2.
Understand dependencies between systems. 3. Produce a concise
implementation architecture. 4. Identify technical risks and
dependencies. 5. Establish the project structure. 6. Define the first
milestone.

Then immediately begin Stage 1. Do not spend the response merely
repeating the concept. Create actual working files and a playable
application.

Implement Stage 1 completely. Do not attempt all six stages in one
untested operation.

When Stage 1 is complete, report: - Files created. - Features
implemented. - Tests performed. - Bugs discovered and fixed. - Remaining
work.

Wait for the next development instruction.

FINAL DIRECTIVE Build LAST NIGHT as a polished, atmospheric,
challenging, genuinely playable 2D survival-horror roguelite.

The player must feel that every night, every bullet, every bandage, and
every decision matters.

Seven hours. One helicopter. One chance.

LAST NIGHT.
